20 Free Things in Ireland Most Immigrants Miss

Free courses, family payments, and career help in Ireland that newcomers often miss. Real entitlements, eligibility, and how to apply.

# **20 Free Things You Can Get in Ireland That Most People Don't Know About**

If you've moved to Ireland from India — or anywhere else — chances are somebody told you about the obvious stuff: register with the GNIB, get a PPS number, open a bank account. What almost nobody tells you is how much *free* support sits behind that PPS number once you have it. Not loans. Not discounts. Free: money, courses, childcare payments, debt advice, energy upgrades, even schoolbooks.

Most of it isn't hidden exactly — it's just written for people who already know the system, in language that assumes you grew up here. A lot of it also *does* extend to visa holders, work permit holders, and even people going through the international protection process, which surprises people who assume "immigrant" automatically means "not eligible." Sometimes it doesn't. We've flagged eligibility for non-EU/EEA nationals specifically for every single item below, because that's usually the first question we get asked.

A few ground rules before you dive in: amounts and thresholds in Ireland change every Budget (usually announced in October, effective the following January), so we've dated everything to 2026 and linked the official source for each figure. Where sources disagreed with each other, we've said so rather than guessing. And anywhere we weren't 100% sure something is still current, we've flagged it in the "double-check" list at the end — please verify before this goes live.

## **Career Help**

### **1. Intreo — Ireland's free public employment service**

**What you get:** A dedicated case officer, CV and interview help, access to JobsIreland.ie job listings, training referrals, and work-experience programmes like the Work Placement Experience Programme (a 6-month voluntary placement for people unemployed 6+ months). Intreo is a single point of contact for both employment supports and income supports — you don't have to be claiming a payment to use the employment side of it[ gov.ie – Intreo](https://www.gov.ie/en/department-of-social-protection/organisation-information/intreo-the-public-employment-services/).

**Who's eligible (immigrants/visa holders):** You do not need to be an Irish or EU citizen to use Intreo's job-search and case-officer services — legal residence is what matters. If you're on a valid employment permit or immigration permission that allows you to live and work in Ireland, you can walk into your local Intreo centre[ Department of Enterprise, Trade and Employment – Immigration and employment permits](https://enterprise.gov.ie/en/what-we-do/workplace-and-skills/employment-permits/employment-permit-eligibility/immigration-employment-permits/). If you also want to claim Jobseeker's Benefit or Allowance (a cash payment), that's a separate eligibility test involving PRSI contributions or the Habitual Residence Condition — Intreo staff can tell you which, if any, you qualify for[ Citizens Information – Migrant workers and unemployment](https://www.citizensinformation.ie/en/moving-country/working-in-ireland/migrant-workers/migrant-workers-and-unemployment/). Note that Intreo has also run dedicated employment services specifically for Beneficiaries of Temporary Protection (Ukraine)[ gov.ie – Intreo Employment Services for Beneficiaries of Temporary Protection](https://www.gov.ie/en/department-of-social-protection/publications/intreo-employment-services-for-beneficiaries-of-temporary-protection/).

**How to apply:** Find your nearest centre via the[ Intreo Centre locator](https://www.gov.ie/en/department-of-social-protection/services/find-your-local-intreo-centre/), bring ID and (if you have one) your PPS number, and ask for a case officer appointment. You can also register on[ JobsIreland.ie](https://jobsireland.ie/en-US/job-seekers) without visiting in person.

⚠️ *Flag: I couldn't confirm from official sources whether a PPS number is a hard requirement just to walk in and get advice, or only required once you apply for a payment. Some secondary sources say "not required to look for work," but I'd verify this directly with an Intreo centre before publishing it as fact.*

## **Free Courses & CV-Boosting**

### **2. Your local library card — and everything it unlocks**

**What you get:** Membership is free at any age, and your card works at every public library in the country[ Libraries Ireland – Join your library](https://www.librariesireland.ie/join-your-library). Beyond borrowing books, your card gets you: free public wifi and PC access; eBooks, eAudiobooks and eMagazines through BorrowBox and similar apps; local studies collections; and free events (book clubs, talks, exhibitions, craft classes)[ SDCC – What can I do with my library card?](https://www.sdcc.ie/en/services/sport-and-recreation/libraries/library-services/ask-a-librarian/what-can-i-do-with-my-library-card-.html). Crucially for this list: your library barcode also gives you free access to over 400 self-paced eLearning courses (business, IT, languages, creative skills) through Universal Class[ Libraries Ireland – eLearning Courses](https://www.librariesireland.ie/elibrary/elearning-courses).

**Who's eligible (immigrants/visa holders):** Yes — library membership in Ireland is not restricted by citizenship or visa type. You'll need photo ID (passport is fine) and proof of current address (a utility bill or bank statement dated within 6 months)[ Libraries Ireland – Join your library](https://www.librariesireland.ie/join-your-library). I could not find an official statement addressing what happens if you can't produce a standard utility bill (common for people in shared housing or direct provision) — worth calling your local branch to ask about alternative proof of address.

**How to apply:** Join online at[ LibrariesIreland.ie](https://www.librariesireland.ie/), or in person at any branch. Online sign-up gets you digital access immediately; you'll need to complete membership in person to borrow physical books.

⚠️ *Flag: Some international library systems (mostly in the US) offer free museum passes through library cards — I found no evidence this exists in the Irish system, and Libraries Ireland's own pages don't mention it. Don't include a museum-pass claim unless you can confirm it locally.*

### **3. SOLAS Skills to Advance — free upskilling if you're already working**

**What you get:** Free or low-cost short courses for people currently employed, particularly in sectors experiencing change, delivered through your local Education and Training Board (ETB)[ SOLAS – Skills to Advance](https://www.solas.ie/programmes/skills-to-advance/). Courses range from digital skills to leadership training for people new to supervisory roles.

**Who's eligible (immigrants/visa holders):** The programme is aimed at people in employment generally — I found no nationality or visa-status restriction stated on the SOLAS or National Enterprise Hub pages, but eligibility criteria vary by course, region, and funding round[ Solas | Skills to Advance | National Enterprise Hub](https://www.neh.gov.ie/business-supports/skills-to-advance). ⚠️ *Flag: I could not find explicit confirmation that work-permit holders (as opposed to EU workers) are eligible for every course — this is worth confirming directly with your local ETB's Skills to Advance coordinator before telling readers they definitely qualify.*

**How to apply:** Search courses via[ Findacourse.ie](https://www.findacourse.ie/skills-to-advance/) or contact your local ETB's Skills to Advance representative directly.

### **4. SOLAS Learning Works / Adult Literacy for Life — free literacy, numeracy and digital skills**

**What you get:** Free courses in reading, writing, maths, and digital literacy, run through 100+ local adult literacy services around the country, delivered one-to-one or in small groups, in person, by phone, or online[ NALA – Free courses](https://www.nala.ie/free-courses/). SOLAS's national Adult Literacy for Life strategy specifically funds projects reaching lone parents, Traveller and Roma communities, refugees, people with disabilities, and the long-term unemployed[ SOLAS – Learning Works](https://www.solas.ie/latest-news/12-transformational-community-projects-showcased-at-literacy-changes-lives-event/).

**Who's eligible (immigrants/visa holders):** These are community education services open to adults broadly, and refugee communities are explicitly named as a target group in SOLAS's own literacy strategy — a strong signal that immigration status isn't a barrier here. That said, I could not find one single official page stating "all visa holders are eligible" in plain terms; if you want to state this with full confidence, it's worth a direct call to NALA.

**How to apply:** Call the NALA Freephone line on **1800 20 20 65**, or contact your nearest ETB adult literacy service directly[ NALA – Free courses](https://www.nala.ie/free-courses/).

### **5. ETB courses — including free English classes (ESOL) for migrants**

**What you get:** Ireland's 16 Education and Training Boards run free Further Education and Training (FET), including Post Leaving Certificate courses, community education, and — importantly for this audience — free English language (ESOL) classes at multiple levels[ Citizens Information – Further education and training courses](https://www.citizensinformation.ie/en/education/further-education-and-training/training-courses/). Note that most national FET programmes are free, but full-time Post Leaving Certificate (PLC) courses carry a standard **€200 participant contribution per year** (some ETBs charge up to €500 including course/exam materials)[ PLC Courses Ireland – Education and Training Boards](https://www.plccourses.ie/education-and-training-boards/) — so "free" doesn't apply uniformly across every ETB course; check before enrolling.

**Who's eligible (immigrants/visa holders):** ESOL classes are explicitly open to migrants. City of Dublin ETB's Adult Education Service states its English classes are for "people who have come to live in Ireland, including asylum seekers and refugees, EU and non-EU economic migrants"[ City of Dublin ETB – English Language Classes](https://adulteducationcityofdublinetb.ie/english-language-classes/). Waterford and Wexford ETB names "refugees, international protection applicants, temporary protection directive applicants, economic migrants and anyone resident in the catchment area"[ Waterford and Wexford ETB – ESOL](https://waterfordwexford.etb.ie/adult-education-services/esol-english-for-speakers-of-other-languages/). Cavan and Monaghan ETB says its programmes are for "migrant workers, the unemployed, asylum seekers, refugees and all other non-nationals" and that all courses are free[ Cavan and Monaghan ETB – ESOL](https://www.cmetb.ie/english-for-speakers-of-other-languages/). This is one of the clearest, best-documented "yes, immigrants qualify" items on this whole list.

**How to apply:** Contact your local ETB's adult education service directly (search "[your county] ETB adult education" or use the[ ETBI directory](https://www.etbi.ie/etbs/)), or search courses nationally via[ FETCH Courses](https://www.fetchcourses.ie/).

### **6. FETCH Courses — the free national portal for enrolling in FET/ETB courses**

**What you get:** A single national account (sometimes referred to informally as a "learner account") for searching and applying to Further Education and Training courses anywhere in Ireland, run by SOLAS with the Education and Training Boards[ FET Course Hub – fetchcourses.ie](https://www.fetchcourses.ie/). Setting up an account and applying through it is free.

**Who's eligible (immigrants/visa holders):** Open to anyone over 16 applying to FET programmes — eligibility depends on the specific course you're applying for, not on the portal itself.

**How to apply:** Register a free account at[ FETCHcourses.ie](https://www.fetchcourses.ie/), search courses, and apply directly online.

## **Family Benefits**

A general note that applies to almost every payment below: most (though not all) require you to satisfy the **Habitual Residence Condition (HRC)** — proof of a genuine, settled connection to Ireland — rather than Irish or EU citizenship specifically. EU/EEA citizens working in Ireland automatically satisfy it; non-EU/EEA nationals need to demonstrate it, and asylum seekers/people in the international protection process are generally *not* regarded as habitually resident, with some specific exceptions noted below[ Citizens Information – The habitual residence condition](https://www.citizensinformation.ie/en/social-welfare/irish-social-welfare-system/social-assistance-payments/habitual-residence-condition/).

### **7. Child Benefit**

**What you get:** €140 a month, per child, paid on the first Tuesday of every month, regardless of your income or PRSI record — it's a universal payment once you qualify. Twins get 1.5× the rate per child; triplets and higher get 2× per child. A New Baby Grant of €280 is paid alongside your first month's Child Benefit for babies born or adopted on or after 1 December 2024[ Citizens Information – Child Benefit](https://www.citizensinformation.ie/en/social-welfare/families-and-children/child-benefit/) (this is the current, up-to-date figure I verified directly on the official page, last edited 27 June 2025 — one secondary source I found quoted €150/month, which appears to be outdated or incorrect; go with €140).

**Who's eligible (immigrants/visa holders):** You must live in Ireland and satisfy the Habitual Residence Condition. If you're an EU/EEA citizen working here, you automatically meet it. If you're a non-EU/EEA citizen legally working in Ireland, you *may* qualify — your child needs to be resident in Ireland (or another EU state) but does not need to be an Irish citizen[ Citizens Information – Child Benefit, "Non-Irish citizens" section](https://www.citizensinformation.ie/en/social-welfare/families-and-children/child-benefit/).

**How to apply:** If your baby is born in Ireland, registering the birth within 3 months automatically starts the process. Otherwise, complete Form CB1 and send it to the Child Benefit Section, Department of Social Protection, Letterkenny, Co. Donegal — details and the form are on the[ gov.ie Child Benefit page](https://www.gov.ie/en/department-of-social-protection/services/child-benefit/). Apply within 12 months of your baby's birth, your family moving to Ireland, or you starting work in Ireland.

### **8. Back to School Clothing and Footwear Allowance**

**What you get:** €160 per child aged 2–11, and €285 per child aged 12+ in second-level education, for 2026. Budget 2026 extended eligibility down to include 2- and 3-year-olds for the first time[ Citizens Information – Back to School Clothing and Footwear Allowance](https://www.citizensinformation.ie/en/social-welfare/families-and-children/back-to-school-clothing-and-footwear-allowance/).

**Who's eligible (immigrants/visa holders):** You need a qualifying social welfare payment or an approved training/employment scheme, plus a means test — the same underlying HRC/PRSI rules apply as for other family payments above.

**How to apply:** Apply online at[ MyWelfare.ie](https://services.mywelfare.ie/en/topics/parents-children-family/back-to-school-clothing-footwear-allowance/). The 2026 scheme opened 3 June 2026, closes 30 September 2026, and payments for early applicants began the week of 13 July 2026[ gov.ie – Back to School Clothing and Footwear Allowance](https://www.gov.ie/en/department-of-social-protection/services/back-to-school-clothing-and-footwear-allowance/).

### **9. Working Family Payment (formerly Family Income Supplement)**

**What you get:** A weekly, tax-free top-up for working parents on lower incomes — renamed from Family Income Supplement (FIS) to Working Family Payment (WFP) some years ago, so if an older relative or forum post mentions FIS, they mean this. The payment is 60% of the difference between your average weekly family income and the income limit for your family size. As of 1 January 2026, weekly income limits are €765 (1 child), €866 (2 children), €967 (3 children), €1,058 (4 children) — Budget 2026 raised these limits by €60/week across all family sizes[ Citizens Information – Working Family Payment](https://www.citizensinformation.ie/en/social-welfare/families-and-children/working-family-payment/). From March 2026, WFP also became a qualifying payment for Fuel Allowance, backdated to January 2026 for those who meet the conditions[ gov.ie – Budget 2026](https://www.gov.ie/en/department-of-social-protection/publications/budget-2026/).

**Who's eligible (immigrants/visa holders):** You must work 38+ hours per fortnight (you can combine hours with a spouse/partner), have at least one qualifying child, and meet the standard HRC test.

**How to apply:** Apply via[ MyWelfare.ie](https://services.mywelfare.ie/en/topics/parents-children-family/working-family-payment/) or your local Intreo centre.

### **10. Maternity Benefit**

**What you get:** €299 a week for 26 weeks (156 days, Sundays excluded), plus the option of a further 16 weeks unpaid leave afterward, during which you may get PRSI credits[ Citizens Information – Maternity Benefit](https://www.citizensinformation.ie/en/social-welfare/families-and-children/maternity-benefit/).

**Who's eligible (immigrants/visa holders):** This is a PRSI-based (contributory) benefit, not an HRC-based one — meaning your citizenship or visa type doesn't matter directly, what matters is your PRSI contribution record. You typically need 39 weeks of PRSI paid since starting work and 39 weeks paid/credited in the relevant tax year (or the year after), or alternatively 26 weeks paid in each of two consecutive relevant tax years. This means a work-permit holder who has been legally employed and paying PRSI in Ireland long enough can qualify exactly like an Irish citizen — no separate immigration test.

**How to apply:** Complete Form MB1 (doctor confirms due date, employer confirms leave dates) and submit at least 6 weeks before leave starts (12 weeks if self-employed)[ Citizens Information – Maternity Benefit](https://www.citizensinformation.ie/en/social-welfare/families-and-children/maternity-benefit/).

### **11. Paternity Benefit**

**What you get:** €299 a week for 2 consecutive weeks, to be taken within the first 26 weeks after the birth or adoption[ Citizens Information – Paternity Benefit](https://www.citizensinformation.ie/en/social-welfare/families-and-children/paternity-benefit/).

**Who's eligible (immigrants/visa holders):** Same PRSI-based logic as Maternity Benefit above — 26 weeks PRSI paid in the relevant tax year and 26 weeks paid in the year before it (employees), or 52 weeks of Class S contributions across three tax years (self-employed). Not dependent on citizenship.

**How to apply:** Apply at least 4 weeks before your intended leave (12 weeks if self-employed), fastest via[ MyWelfare.ie](https://services.mywelfare.ie/en/topics/parents-children-family/paternity-benefit/) — you'll need a verified MyGovID.

### **12. Parent's Leave and Benefit**

**What you get:** 9 weeks of paid leave per parent, at €299 a week, to be used within the child's first 2 years (or within 2 years of an adoption placement). Parents can split or share how they take their weeks[ Citizens Information – Parent's Benefit](https://www.citizensinformation.ie/en/social-welfare/families-and-children/parents-benefit/).

**Who's eligible (immigrants/visa holders):** PRSI-based, same structure as Maternity/Paternity Benefit above — 39 weeks PRSI since starting work plus 39 weeks in the relevant year (or the alternative 26+26 week test).

**How to apply:** Notify your employer at least 6 weeks before your intended leave, and apply for the payment via MyWelfare.ie[ Citizens Information – Parent's Benefit](https://www.citizensinformation.ie/en/social-welfare/families-and-children/parents-benefit/).

### **13. School Meals Programme (including Hot School Meals)**

**What you get:** Free daily meals for children at participating primary schools. All DEIS and non-DEIS primary schools became eligible to apply for hot meals as of 2025, with a phased national rollout continuing into 2026; secondary schools are not currently eligible for the hot meals element[ Citizens Information – School Meals Scheme](https://www.citizensinformation.ie/en/education/the-irish-education-system/school-meals-scheme/);[ gov.ie – Minister Humphreys announces roll-out of Hot School Meals](https://www.gov.ie/en/department-of-social-protection/press-releases/minister-humphreys-announces-plans-for-roll-out-of-hot-school-meals-to-all-primary-schools/).

**Who's eligible (immigrants/visa holders):** This isn't something you apply for as a parent — the *school* applies for scheme funding, and if your child's school participates, your child gets the meals automatically, regardless of your immigration status. Your only real "application" is choosing/confirming a school that's part of the scheme and, in some schools, submitting a weekly food order as of changes made in March 2026.

**How to apply:** Ask your child's school (or a school you're considering) whether they participate in the School Meals Scheme / Hot School Meals. Schools apply for funding annually between May and the end of October.

⚠️ *Flag: rollout to "all primary schools" was still being discussed/committee-reviewed as of January 2026 per an RTÉ report on sustainability concerns — I'd confirm current coverage in your area rather than promising every school has it*[* RTÉ – School hot meal scheme 'not sustainable', cttee hears*](https://www.rte.ie/news/politics/2026/0114/1552967-hot-school-meals-committee/)*.*

### **14. One-Parent Family Payment**

**What you get:** From January 2026, the maximum weekly personal rate is €230 (up €10 from €220), for parents under 66, not cohabiting, whose youngest child is under 7[ Citizens Information – Social welfare rates 2026](https://www.citizensinformation.ie/en/money-and-tax/budgets/social-welfare-rates-announced-in-budget-2026/);[ Citizens Information – One-Parent Family Payment](https://www.citizensinformation.ie/en/social-welfare/families-and-children/one-parent-family-payment/). The first €165 a week of your own earnings is disregarded (not counted) when your payment is calculated.

**Who's eligible (immigrants/visa holders):** Standard HRC test applies — you must live in Ireland and demonstrate a genuine settled connection here.

**How to apply:** Apply through[ MyWelfare.ie](https://www.gov.ie/en/department-of-social-protection/services/one-parent-family-payment/) or your local Intreo/Social Welfare office.

### **15. Household Benefits Package**

**What you get:** €35 a month toward your electricity or gas bill, plus a free TV licence (worth €160/year)[ Selectra – Household Benefits Package 2026](https://selectra.ie/energy/guides/schemes/household-benefits-package);[ Citizens Information – Household Benefits Package](https://www.citizensinformation.ie/en/social-welfare/extra-social-welfare-benefits/household-benefits-package/). Only one package per household, and it's electricity *or* gas, not both.

**Who's eligible (immigrants/visa holders):** This one's mainly for older residents and people with disabilities rather than the average working-age newcomer — automatic (non-means-tested) from age 70, possible from 66–70 on a qualifying payment, and available under 66 if you're getting Carer's Allowance, Disability Allowance, Blind Pension, or a Bereaved Partner's Pension (aged 60–65). It's included here because it's genuinely underused by people who *do* qualify (e.g., a parent or grandparent who's joined you in Ireland on a Stamp 0/long-stay visa and is receiving a qualifying Irish payment) but don't know it exists.

**How to apply:** Apply online via[ MyWelfare.ie](https://www.gov.ie/en/department-of-social-protection/services/household-benefits-package/) or by post.

## **Money & Housing Help**

### **16. MABS — free, confidential debt and budgeting advice**

**What you get:** One-to-one help from a qualified money adviser to build a budget, negotiate with creditors, and work out a debt repayment plan — completely free and confidential, with no judgment[ MABS – How MABS Helps](https://www.mabs.ie/en/about/how-we-help/).

**Who's eligible (immigrants/visa holders):** MABS presents itself as a universal, free public service with no stated citizenship or immigration restriction. I could not find an official MABS page that explicitly says "immigrants and visa holders are eligible" in so many words — the absence of any restriction is a good sign, but if you want to state eligibility with total certainty, it's worth a quick call to confirm there's no residency requirement attached.

**How to apply:** Call the MABS Helpline on **0818 07 2000** (Mon–Fri, 9am–8pm), or find your nearest office at[ MABS.ie](https://www.mabs.ie/en/).

### **17. Additional Needs Payment (formerly Exceptional/Urgent Needs Payment)**

**What you get:** A one-off payment toward an essential cost you can't cover from your weekly income or savings — things like emergency clothing, bedding, cooking utensils when setting up a first home, or funeral-related costs[ Citizens Information – Additional Needs Payment](https://www.citizensinformation.ie/en/social-welfare/supplementary-welfare-schemes/additional-needs-payment/). There's no fixed amount — it's assessed case by case by a Community Welfare Officer, and there's no automatic entitlement even if you meet the general criteria.

**Who's eligible (immigrants/visa holders):** This is one of the more genuinely inclusive items on this list. People in full-time education, full-time employment, or involved in a trade dispute are normally excluded — but critically, asylum seekers living in direct provision *can* apply, for example for help buying school uniforms, even though they don't meet the Habitual Residence Condition that blocks them from other payments[ Citizens Information – Income support for asylum seekers](https://www.citizensinformation.ie/en/moving-country/asylum-seekers-and-refugees/services-for-asylum-seekers-in-ireland/income-support-for-asylum-seekers/).

**How to apply:** Apply online via[ MyWelfare.ie](https://services.mywelfare.ie/en/topics/community-welfare-services/additional-needs-payment/) (needs a verified MyGovID), by post, or by booking an appointment with your local Community Welfare Officer.

### **18. Warmer Homes Scheme — free home energy upgrades**

**What you get:** Fully-funded insulation, draught-proofing, efficient lighting and (where eligible) renewable heating upgrades for homeowners on qualifying payments, run by SEAI. If you qualify, there's nothing to pay[ SEAI – The Warmer Homes Scheme](https://www.seai.ie/grants/home-energy-grants/fully-funded-upgrades-for-certain-homeowners).

**Who's eligible (immigrants/visa holders):** You must own and live in the home (built before 2006, BER rating C or below), and be receiving a qualifying social welfare payment such as Fuel Allowance. **Important limitation for renters:** tenants cannot apply directly — this scheme is restricted to owner-occupiers. If you're renting, your landlord would need to apply under a separate, less generous landlord scheme[ Citizens Information – Warmer Homes Scheme](https://www.citizensinformation.ie/en/housing/housing-grants-and-schemes/grants-for-home-renovations-and-improvements/warmer-homes-scheme/). Since most newly arrived immigrants rent rather than own, this is genuinely useful mainly for people who've bought a home or for relatives who own — worth knowing so you don't waste time on an application you're not eligible for.

**How to apply:** Apply online at[ SEAI.ie](https://www.seai.ie/grants/home-energy-grants/fully-funded-upgrades-for-certain-homeowners) or by downloading the application form; SEAI will do a free assessment if you don't already have a BER rating.

## **Bonus Finds**

Two more genuinely useful, free, and underused entitlements that came up repeatedly in our research but didn't fit neatly into the categories above.

### **19. Free Schoolbooks Scheme**

**What you get:** Free schoolbooks, workbooks and core classroom resources for every child in a recognised primary or special school (since 2023), and now — as of the 2025/26 school year — every student in a recognised post-primary school too, covering Junior Cycle, Senior Cycle, and Transition Year. Almost one million students are covered nationally, with no means test[ gov.ie – Minister McEntee announces final phase of free schoolbooks scheme](https://www.gov.ie/en/department-of-education/press-releases/minister-mcentee-announces-final-phase-of-free-schoolbooks-scheme/).

**Who's eligible (immigrants/visa holders):** Universal — every student at a participating recognised school qualifies, with no citizenship, visa, or income test. This is arguably the single most "no catch" item on this entire list.

**How to apply:** Nothing to apply for — funding goes directly to the school. If your child's school is still asking you to buy books, check whether the school is part of the Free Education Scheme via the[ Department of Education](https://www.gov.ie/en/department-of-education/publications/primary-schoolbooks-scheme/), or ask the school directly.

*(Source found during general research on family/education entitlements, not in your original brief — flagging per your "bonus find" instructions.)*

### **20. GP Visit Card for children under 8**

**What you get:** Free GP visits for your child (regular visits, out-of-hours urgent visits, asthma care), plus free preventive medical checks at ages 2 and 5, until the end of the month your child turns 8[ Citizens Information – GP visit cards for children under 8](https://www.citizensinformation.ie/en/health/medical-cards-and-gp-visit-cards/gp-visit-cards-for-under-8s/).

**Who's eligible (immigrants/visa holders):** Your child must be "ordinarily resident" — living in Ireland and intending to stay at least a year. This is a lower, simpler bar than the Habitual Residence Condition used for cash payments, and it is not tied to citizenship, so most immigrant families with children under 8 should qualify once settled.

**How to apply:** Apply on the[ HSE website](https://www2.hse.ie/services/schemes-allowances/gp-visit-cards/under-8s/) — you'll need your PPS number and your child's. Check your GP participates in the scheme before applying, print the confirmation email, and get your GP to sign it before posting it back.
